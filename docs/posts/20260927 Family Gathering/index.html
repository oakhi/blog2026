Family Gathering

gatherings
Author

DK

Published

September 27, 2026

Returning from a western city road trip follows a unwritten script: minimum pit stops and pack some snacks in order to get home as fast as legally possible.

We seldom consider sitting down for a meal, since that would negate the minutes saved traveling at allowable speeds—hence the importance of snacks consumed in the car.

On this particular visit, we decided to join the group just two days before the event.

Because of the short planning horizon, we weren’t even at our destination for twenty-four hours, and we didn’t plan any sightseeing.

The mission of this trip was simple: to get better acquainted with the children of our nieces and nephews—kids we seldom see, let alone interact with.

A Setting Built for Connection

Staying at a hotel is convenient but it isolates families into separate rooms, but choosing a short-term rental proved to be the right choice.

The layout was built for interactions: three bedrooms on the west side, four more on the east, with common areas; kitchen, dining and family rooms. And all thirteen beds would be occupied by the visiting groups.

In total, there were ten children under one roof, ranging in age from nine down to one, with clusters at ages six and four.

Watching them interact was a reminder in human nature.

The children knew how to associate instinctively—falling into games, laughter, and shared spaces without hesitation or awkwardness.

The adults, on the other hand, had to relearn how to do it.

We were busy getting involved in a shared game room, an outdoor pool, and a bubbling hot tub.

It provided the grown-ups a chance to learn again how to be around children at play.

The Cost of Gathering

Even in a city as large and diverse as Las Vegas, securing a reservation for thirty people was a challenge.

We were told to confirm our table an hour before arrival, only to be reminded of a spending minimum upon calling.

When we finally arrived, we were asked to wait twenty minutes—which stretched closer to an hour before we finally tasted some food.

Yet, once the food began to flow, it was exceptional.

Enhanced by the atmosphere created by the arrangement of children seated in bench seats into their newly formed groups, facing their parents.

With the grandparents in the middle section of the room, flanked by 2 tables with grandchildren.

When the check arrived, it rivaled the cost of the last used car I purchased. I didn’t feel the price was high.

More importantly, we had gathered in one place to reaffirm our bonds as descendants of our parents.

The Road Home

The next morning brought a slow, family by family checkout process, each leaving for their next destination.

Someone had ordered bagels and cream cheese for a light send-off.

With no more plans left, we waited until everyone else had departed, locked the front door, and hit the road.

We planned our pit stops and estimated our arrival time at home as we pulled out of Clark County.

But then, after two and a half hours of driving—just as we passed Cedar City near mile marker 37—we received a phone call from a group that had left hours before us.

They told us they were waiting at a rest stop about thirty minutes ahead. Furthermore, they had stopped by a Korean market along the way.

As we were getting hungry, it was the most welcome message imaginable.

It was the fourth daughter and her husband, thoughtfully looking out for the second and third daughters and their husbands.

They were standing outside, waving as we pulled into the rest stop.

All the Korean favorites were laid out: 떡, 김밥, katsu, and pastries.

We hadn’t planned to eat like this, and we certainly weren’t expecting it.

However, there along the I-15, between mile markers 87 and 88, we were fed with food that we had grown up with.

This weekend I felt the importance of gathering of family members.

The continuation and confirmation of linkage between generations.

Both the family you are born into and the family you marry into.
